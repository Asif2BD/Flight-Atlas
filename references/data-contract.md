# Flight Atlas ledger v1

Flight Atlas by M Asif Rahman · [AR.bd](https://AR.bd)
JSON object: `schema_version: 1`, `report`, `legs`, optional `ground_segments` and `manual_gaps`.

`report`: title, start_date and end_date (inclusive YYYY-MM-DD), as_of (ISO date), inclusion_mode (`reconstructed` default or `confirmed`), coverage_note (sources searched and limitations in safe prose). Scope is origin-local departure date. Never include future flights in historical totals, even in a full-year requested window.

Flights the person states themselves (no email) use `status: user_confirmed` and opaque `evidence_ids` such as `user-statement-1`; the analyzer excludes legs with no evidence reference.

Each `legs` entry requires: id, traveler_id (pseudonym), departure_date, origin and destination airport codes (case is normalised), status from evidence.md, and evidence_ids (private opaque references; a leg without one is excluded with a warning). Recommended: operating_flight (needed for codeshare checks), marketing_flights (list), airline, evidence_note, departure_scheduled and arrival_scheduled (ISO date-time with explicit UTC offsets), scheduled_minutes (integer or null), duration_basis (`ticket_advertised`, `historical_schedule`, `schedule_calculated`, `estimated`), duration_resolution (if a discrepancy was explicitly resolved), duplicate_of, supersedes_id, origin_lat/lon, destination_lat/lon, origin_country and destination_country (ISO codes or consistently named territories). Coordinates require verified airport reference data; missing coordinates omit the route from the map, not the flight ledger.

Report fields: `title`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `as_of`, optional `inclusion_mode`, `coverage_note`, and `home_airports` (list of IATA codes; trips start and end there; inferred and confirmed with the person when absent).

Answers to the trip questions go in `manual_gaps` with the open leg's `leg_id` and a `kind` starting `outbound_` (before the first leg) or `return_` (after the last), for example `outbound_before_period`, `return_overland`, `return_unknown`, plus a short `note`. A mid-trip gap explained by ground travel is a `ground_segments` entry with `after_id`/`before_id`. Flights read from an uploaded ticket, boarding pass or screenshot are ordinary rows with opaque `evidence_ids` such as `upload-2026-11-10-1`.

The summary adds `trips` (id, dates, route, flights, minutes, status and flags; `home_change` when the trip leaves from a different home than the last one ended at, `continues` when it runs past the report end), `home_airports`, `home_inferred` and `clarifications` (the open questions as data; `clarify.py` words them). `places` lists `cities` and `countries` stayed in (with `home` flags and first dates), `transit_airports` and `transit_only_countries`; metrics add `visited_city_count`, `visited_country_count` and `transit_only_country_count`. Trip statuses: `complete`, `between_homes`, `outbound_missing`, `return_missing`, `gap`, `explained`, and the notes `return_after_snapshot`, `return_after_period`, `outbound_before_period`. Keep the traveler's own flights just outside the report dates in the ledger: they are excluded from totals but tell the analyzer a trip began earlier or continues later.

Enrichment input (optional, recommended): `departure_local` and `arrival_local` are the wall-clock times printed on the ticket (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM`, no offset, arrival with its own date). `scripts/enrich.py INPUT --out OUTPUT` upper-cases IATA codes, fills missing lat/lon/country from `assets/airports.csv`, adds `origin_tz`/`destination_tz` (IANA), converts local times to offset-aware `*_scheduled` values, fills a missing `departure_date`, and warns when existing coordinates, countries or offsets disagree with the reference. It keeps existing values unless `--overwrite` is passed. Unknown codes are left for manual, verified entry.

The analyzer accepts an advertised duration without timestamps, or calculates from two offset-aware timestamps. Naive timestamps and contradictory departure dates are rejected for timing. Resolve local IANA time-zone conversion before populating ISO offsets; preserve original dates/zones in private source metadata. Treat more than 30 hours or <=0 minutes as invalid pending investigation.

Partial date records (`2025-09`, unknown day) are retained as unresolved exclusions, not forced onto a date. If the user confirms the precise date, update with provenance and re-run. Exact-date legs with missing durations still count in the included flight count; no hours are assigned.

Each `ground_segments` item: id, from, to (same airport/location identifiers as the linked flights), after_id and before_id (exact flight IDs), status (`user_confirmed` or `documented` for closure), mode, note. Optional `via`: the stops on the way, each `{city, country (ISO alpha-2), lat, lon, date}` (a bus Vienna → Prague → Berlin lists Prague); they count as cities visited. Keep inferred ground transfers as manual gaps, not closure. Ground entries never affect flight totals.

`manual_gaps`: objects containing kind, note, optional after_id/before_id/from/to. Kind `hint` answers nothing: its note (what a search found, such as an arrival card, a hotel or a taxi from an airport) is shown with the matching question so the person can answer in a word. Useful kinds include missing_flight, unknown_duration, open_trip, boundary_context, date_unknown, unverified_ground. Use `boundary_context` for out-of-range flights, not duplicated included legs.

Analyzer output: report, metrics, included_legs, monthly, annual, airlines, routes, gaps, exclusions, warnings, status_counts, duration_basis_counts and longest_legs. Metrics are recorded-flight totals; countries count airport countries including transit. Estimated distance is spherical great-circle airport distance, not actual flown mileage. `known_duration_count` excludes unknown and estimated durations. Output summary is sanitized to selected display fields; retain the source ledger separately.

Run `analyze.py INPUT --out OUTPUT`, then review warnings; do not treat successful execution as semantic verification. It does not read email or resolve passenger identity automatically. An unresolved duplicate or timing conflict must be investigated before presenting a final result.
